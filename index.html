01 · ABOUT US 队伍介绍

Student-Led. Engineering-Driven. Community-Focused.

Team 8347 began the 2025–2026 season as a rookie team. Our goal: grow into a mature team with a complete engineering system, cutting-edge technical accumulation, and a real impact on our community.

学生主导 · 工程设计 · STEM 推广

“More than a robot” — 8347 is built by students who love engineering, programming and communication, and who believe that failure is an essential part of the engineering process.

8347 is a student-led and student-managed FIRST Tech Challenge team. Throughout the season we focus on applying the engineering design process to build a reliable, competitive robot — while promoting STEM education in our community.

Every member is encouraged to speak up. Even ideas that look immature at first are worth trying and improving together. Through testing and iteration we learned that failure is not the end — it is an indispensable part of engineering design.

Beyond the robot, we want to pass the passion for engineering and technology to more students. We believe FTC is not just about building robots — it is about teamwork, responsibility, and the ability to solve problems.

01.1 · TEAM LEGACY 校队传承

A Legacy That Paused — and Restarted

WFLA × FTC · 2012—2020

WFLA FTC Roots

WFLA has competed in FTC since 2012. In 2018, two teams from The BOLTS won the alliance championship at the Shanghai Invitational — the school's fourth FTC title since the event arrived in China in 2011. After the COVID pause in 2020, 8347 Cosmic Singularity brings the WFLA flag back to the FTC field.

WHY THIS NAME

Why “Cosmic Singularity”?

Inspired by Chuansha No.2 High School's “Absolute Zero”, we think physical limits make a cool team name. Absolute Zero is the limit of temperature — and time, too, has an origin: the Big Bang. Cosmic Singularity, the origin of everything, symbolizes a team that starts from nothing and is bound to achieve something brilliant.

01.4 · CHALLENGES 主要挑战

What We Learned by Falling

A一
Academics vs. training time
Mid-season exam pressure slowed build and test progress. Fixes: earlier plans, parallel task splitting, shared docs + WeChat sync to keep development steady.
B二
Cross-team communication
Mechanical changes didn't always reach programming in time, causing rework. Fixes: stage summaries, a unified engineering log, shared issue trackers, live updates in the group chat.

Engineering design is not only about building the robot — it needs stable communication, continuous iteration, and the ability to keep adjusting when problems appear.

02 · MECHANICAL ENGINEERING 机械工程

Rebuilt from the Ground Up for the Playoffs

Absorbing the strengths of world-class teams while keeping the best of our national-season machine, we refreshed the entire design and technical route for the playoff robot.

头脑风暴 → 原型搭建 → 测试 → 优化迭代
02.1 · DESIGN PHILOSOPHY 工程设计理念
STEP 01Brainstorming

头脑风暴 — explore concepts and routes before touching CAD.

STEP 02Prototype Building

原型搭建 — build quickly to validate ideas with real hardware.

STEP 03Testing & Data

测试与数据采集 — measure what works and what breaks.

STEP 04Iteration & Optimization

优化迭代 — repeat until the design earns its place on the field.

02.2 · DESIGN METHOD 设计方法论
STEP 1

Playoff Goals

Target maximum total balls: drop the colored-RP goal, accelerate transport and shooting for more shots on the field.

STEP 2

CAD Design

Sketch the whole machine, confirm ball path and dimensions, and model every part 1:1 for assembly sizing.

STEP 3

Prototype Validation

Laser-cut acrylic prototypes so assemblers learn the order and cautions before final parts arrive.

STEP 4

Iteration

Test every module of the prototype and iterate module by module to lock the final solution.

STEP 5

Final Assembly

Machine the final parts, complete final assembly and full-system testing.

02.3 · ROBOT SPECS 整机参数

Actuators 执行器8 motors · 3 servos
Sensors 传感器Encoder ×1 · Pinpoint field-localization ×1 · Limelight

Intake 吸球Secondary intake with rubber-tube wheels for generous deformation — grab, then feed, boosting intake efficiency.
02.4 · BALL PATH ITERATIONS 球路迭代

Four Versions on the Road to a Clean Ball Path

V1

Compact but Jamming

Compact structure with continuous ball contact — yet transfer rollers were too scattered and the primary intake deformed too much to hold 2/3 of a ball, causing serious jams.

Issue: 卡球风险高
V2

Smoother Intake

Intake squeeze became more reasonable and ball pickup smoother — but the small flywheels were too dense and the big wheel's deformation exceeded the Rhino rubber thickness, failing the whole path.

Issue: 飞轮形变量超限
V3

No More Jams

Secondary intake to transfer now connected smoothly with no jamming — but the whole robot exceeded the height limit, so the solution was abandoned.

Issue: 超高度限制
V4 · FINAL最终版

Balanced Final Path

Reasonable deformation avoids stuck balls; the chute length avoids accidental double-feeding fouls. Remaining risk: the gap between transfer rollers and the big wheel leaves a feed-probability window.

Accepted: 小概率供球隐患
02.5 · SHOOTER ITERATIONS 炮台迭代
V1

Simple, but Unstable

Easy structure and maintenance — but the hex shaft was held only by magnetic force and slipped, the big wheel fixed loosely on its axis, bearings popped out, and the trigger sat too high.

Issue: 固定与摩擦问题
V2

More Stable

Rear small flywheel switched from DIY rollers to small rubber wheels — better friction and concentricity. But assembly took too long, the center of gravity sat forward, and the Limelight angle risked missing AprilTags.

Issue: 装配难 · 重心靠前
V3

Final Solution

Low-profile servo improves trigger blocking; dual-servo hood adjusts faster; screws now secure the sync pulley to the motor. Only the counterweight-linked flywheel remains to keep rotational inertia.

Accepted: 阻力问题解决
02.6 · TURRET IN DEPTH 炮台深析

The 360° turret was sized by math against the field dimensions: a 13.7:1 GOBILDA motor with a 1:5 gear-up keeps turret speed reaching 130% of target RPM during run-and-gun, with ample redundancy.

  • H-bearing supports the whole turret — more convenient and more compact than thrust–radial–thrust stacks.
  • Trade-off: flange sliding friction costs more energy than rolling bearings, slightly hindering rotation.
  • Cable drag chain manages all turret wiring; stiff stock cables replaced with 18 AWG soft silicone wire to minimize drag.
  • Special thanks to team 19705 for the drag-chain and mount source files.
// counterweighted flywheel → 4× equivalent inertia
E = ½Jω² · Jeq = JL (ωL/ωm)²
102 g stainless steel on the small-flywheel shaft → 4× equivalent inertia at 2:1 gear ratio
03 · PROGRAMMING 程序工程

From Spaghetti OpMode to Command-Based Architecture

As the robot grew more complex, the traditional OpMode style became coupled, hard to maintain and slow to debug. For the playoffs we introduced the FTCLib Command-Based architecture — modular software engineering for the whole season ahead.

The Command-Based architecture splits the robot into independent Subsystems and reusable Commands — raising code reuse, team collaboration efficiency, and maintainability across future seasons.

Each mechanism is encapsulated as one subsystem: drive, turret, shooter, intake, pinpoint and limelight. Behaviors become commands that can be composed in both TeleOp and Autonomous.

03.1 · SUBSYSTEM LAYER — 六大子系统
DriveSubsystem

Mecanum chassis motion control · field-centric driving logic.

TurretSubsystem

Turret angle control with PID closed loop: set target, read angle, auto-track, hold heading.

REF: FTC-27570 DECODE HOUSTON
ShooterSubsystem

Flywheel RPM control with feedforward to cancel static friction and back-EMF; interpolation tuning pinpoints problem points under pressure.

IntakeSubsystem

Artifact intake and transport control.

PinpointSubsystem

Relative position via the official Pinpoint API.

LimelightSubsystem

Absolute position from AprilTag vision.

03.2 · COMMAND DESIGN 指令设计
CMD 01

IntakeCommand

Starts the intake, keeps it running, and stops automatically when finished.

CMD 02

ShootCommand

Checks the shooter has reached target RPM, triggers the launch mechanism, then completes.

CMD 03

AimTurretCommand

Computes the target direction, drives the turret, and ends once the target angle is reached.

Commands also prepare the team for the autonomous phase:

AUTO SEQUENCE → AIM → SPIN UP SHOOTER → SHOOT
03.3 · ROBOTCONTAINER & TELEMETRY 统一管理与调试
RobotContainer

Control center for TeleOp: creates all subsystems, registers all commands, maps driver-controller buttons, and manages trigger logic — replacing deep if-else nesting.

Live Telemetry

A full telemetry system shows Robot Pose, Turret Angle, Shooter RPM, PID Error and Match State in real time.

Dashboard

Observe the robot's running state and locate problems quickly during practice.

Results

Modular development with fewer code conflicts, faster debugging, reusable commands across TeleOp & Auto — a foundation for future seasons.

Two goals define the next step for the programming team:

First, an entirely in-house localization system — Limelight regularly scans AprilTags for absolute position, fused with Pinpoint and drivetrain motor-speed integration, so the robot always knows where it is on the field. Second, a Command-Based Autonomous that scores 9 balls.

LOCALIZATION AprilTag + Pinpoint + Motor Integration AUTO 9 Balls via CommandBase

5.3

Mentor Support

We keep expanding mentor and technical resources through cross-team exchange, community events and school promotion. Mentors provide experience, materials and design advice; students own the design, code and daily operations.

A team-wide engineering knowledge base organizing CAD designs, program code, test records, match reviews and the engineering notebook — sorted and preserved long-term, so newcomers onboard fast and information is never lost.

Team Scouting Website

Our own scouting platform records real-time match data, analyzes team performance and backs everything up — making match analysis and strategy far more efficient. We plan to open parts of our engineering resources to other FTC teams.

Daily Team Management
Following the advice of Chuansha No.2 High School 19656, we adopted Feishu as our core collaboration tool — task lists per group, activity check-ins, and shared notes on department cautions and match schedules.
5.6
Transparent Team Finance
Costs are carried by team students, so clear bookkeeping matters. A Feishu spreadsheet lists every purchase from outreach, mechanical and programming groups — date, buyer, personal vs. team items — and computes each member's share fairly.
